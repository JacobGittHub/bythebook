The sign-in page, four ways

A and B keep the book closed: you sign in on its cover, and it opens. C and D lay the sign-in out on the desk beside the book. Each one works: switch between signing in, making an account and resetting a password, then send the form to see what happens next. Nothing is sent anywhere.

The lending card

A library book's pocket glued to the cover, with its card standing up out of it. You fill in the card in typewriter type. When you send it, a date stamp lands on the card and the card drops back into the pocket. Guests skip the card and read in the reading room, which is how a guest's books already work: they don't leave this browser.

C

The desk

A loose page

The plan's own idea: one page lying on the desk, with the closed book beside it. It's a page of the book, with the same running head, rules, folio and Settings rows as every other page. The three forms are bookmarks folded over its top edge, the way each page carries its bookmark. When you sign in, the page slides into the book.

My pick

Build C, the loose page, and give it A's ending: when you sign in, the page goes into the closed book and the cover opens onto the Overview. C is the plan's own step and costs the least. A's cover opening gives the moment of arriving at the book, and the one-page form already covers phones.
