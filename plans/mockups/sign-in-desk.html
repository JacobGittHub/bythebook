The bookplate, on a desk

The cover from round one's A, now a fixed size. It only ever signs you in. Creating an account and resetting a password happen on separate sheets, and these mockups try three ways to bring a sheet in: laid over the cover, torn off a legal pad beside the book, or with the camera moving across to the pad. The last one shows the dashboard on the same desk. Light is oak and dark is walnut, following the theme.

B1

Your second option

A sheet laid over the cover

The cover never changes. “Create an account” slides an invitation card onto the book, over the bookplate, with its invite code at the top. “Forgot your password?” brings an index card instead. “Back to signing in” takes the sheet away again. The sheets can wait on the desk beside the book, so you can see them before you ask, or stay out of sight until you do.

Sheets before they're asked for:

  • No camera: everything happens where you're already looking, and it works the same on a phone, because the sheet fits on the cover.
  • The invitation suits the beta: an account needs an invite code, so the sheet that makes one is an invitation.
  • Cost: the bookplate is covered rather than replaced. That's what you asked for, but it means two layers of paper on the cover at once.
B2

Your first option

The legal pad beside the book

A yellow legal pad lies to the book's right, its top page full of scratch notes on openings. Ask to create an account and that page tears off and falls onto the desk, revealing the form written on the page underneath. The pad is big enough to show the form where it lies, so on a wide window the camera doesn't move. On a phone the pad sits at the desk's edge and the camera goes to it (B3).

  • Lived in by design: the notes are the clutter, and a torn page stays on the desk afterwards.
  • Size: book and pad side by side need about 760px of width. Below that, this turns into B3.
  • Handwriting: the notes use Caveat, a handwriting face. That's a fourth font beyond D2's three; the form's fields stay in the book's fonts.
B3

Your first option, with the camera

The camera moves to the pad

The pad waits at the desk's right edge, mostly out of view. Asking to create an account slides the desk across until the pad is in the middle, then tears off the notes. “Back to signing in” slides back to the book. This lets you judge whether the move feels odd. It's the same kind of camera move as the Library's zoom, but sideways.

  • Where it helps: on a phone there's no room beside the book, so B1 or B3 is needed there whatever happens on a wide window.
  • What it costs: the camera has to move twice for each detour. Also, the desk moving under a still lamp light looks right, but only because the light isn't painted onto the wood.

Chapter II of VIII
  • The strip sits on the wood. Its text needs a dark ink on oak and a light one on walnut. Both pass AA here, but the grain behind lowers readability. The highlighted tab gets a paper chip so it stays clear.
  • The desk is painted behind the strip to hide a leaf turning under it (turns.css). With wood, the strip and the desk have to share one fixed background so the grain lines up.
  • The book never moves on its desk, so objects at the edges stay put from page to page. They'd only appear where the window leaves room, never under the book.

Open questions

  1. Is wood the base look or a vibe? Vibes D14 says vibes leave the desk alone, so wood behind the app would be a change to book-ui's base look (D2/D3), not a vibe. Or should only the sign-in page get the wood, with the app keeping its plain desk?
  2. How lived in? Should the objects be decoration only (hidden from screen readers, never clickable), or should some do something, like the mug opening Settings? I'd keep them decorative.
  3. Are the objects the same every visit? They could be, or the pad's notes could come from your own books, and the pencilled line from the catalog.
  4. A fourth font? Caveat for the handwriting, or keep the notes in the book's fonts in italic?
  5. Phones: is the camera move (B3) fine there even if a wide window uses B1 or B2?
  6. Should the sheets wait on the desk? In B1, do you want to see the invitation before you ask for it, or keep a tidy desk?
