Paperback layouts

Four ways to build the Paperback look into the structure of the page, from a light touch to an open book. None of them has a sidebar box, and the stacked-page edges are gone. The paper grain and the book serif are switches, so you can see how much of the bookishness comes from the layout alone.

The same in all four

  • No sidebar box. The navigation sits on the page or the desk around it.
  • Fewer boxes inside the page. Sections are split by rules or tinted blocks, and the only filled boxes left are the board and the tree views.
  • The red ribbon marks where you are.
  • The board, the book views and the light, middle and dark move bars don't change.
  • The page fits the window, and only the side panels scroll, as the Explorer does today.

What this means for vibes

Every layout here has a desk: the space around the page. That's the natural home for a vibe's art, since it never covers the board or text, so the base UI can stay calm and bookish while the vibe dresses the desk. The page itself takes only color and type from a vibe.

Thumb index and Spread change components, not just tokens: the navigation moves, so the dashboard frame, its phone menu and --dash-offset would change with it.
