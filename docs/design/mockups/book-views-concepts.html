Book display concepts

Five ways to draw a book as a tree that shares the screen with a board, laid out left to right so depth gets the room. Each one draws the same real book: the Queen's Gambit pages of Wikibooks' Chess Opening Theory, 198 positions in 62 lines, the longest 23 plies. Hover a move to preview it on the board, and click it to select it. The book is played as White, so an amber ring marks each position where White has more than one book move.

A

Ply columns

Every position as a dot, one column per ply, so depth lines up across branches.

  • Showsevery position, with branch thickness for how much of the book is behind it.
  • Costsheight: each line needs a row, so labels fit only on the bigger branches.
  • Fitsthe Library and the Bookstore's detail view, where the tree has room.
B

Branch points

Only the places where the book splits. A run of single moves becomes one labelled stretch.

  • Showsthe decisions. Branches under 4 positions fold into "+n" until a selected line opens them.
  • Costsa move in the middle of a run isn't its own target; its stretch is.
  • Fitsbig books near the position limit, and editing, where the splits are what you act on.
C

Metro map

Each line as a route with stations. The main line runs straight, and each defense keeps its color.

  • Showsa place to remember: "the Slav is the blue line, the Accepted branches off at 2.c4".
  • Costsroutes shift more than the other layouts when a book changes, and bushy books cross.
  • Fitsthe Bookstore's detail view and memorability, the product goal.
D

Icicle

Each move as a block whose height is its share of the book's lines: the Labyrinth's regions, laid flat.

  • Showsproportions at a glance: defenses are bands, light and dark blocks alternate by side.
  • Coststhin lines become slivers, and move names fit only in the taller blocks.
  • FitsBookstore cards and Library thumbnails, and comparing books side by side.
E

Spine and ribs

One line straight across, its alternatives as ribs sized by what is behind them. Click a rib to walk it.

  • Showswhere you are and what else the book answers, with the rest of the line dotted ahead.
  • Costsone line at a time; the book's overall shape isn't visible.
  • Fitsthe Explorer's sidebar, as a short strip in place of today's mini tree.
Data: the Queen's Gambit pages of Wikibooks' Chess Opening Theory (CC BY-SA 4.0), one position per page, replayed with chess.js.
